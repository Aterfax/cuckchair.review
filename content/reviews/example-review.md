The **Royal Cuck‑Chair 2.0** is a bold statement in office ergonomics. Its angled design promises a superior viewing experience, but does it live up to the hype? Let's break down each category.

## Position and sightlines

The angled seat puts your eyes directly on the screen with minimal neck tilt. Sightlines are clear from the primary position, though shifting to a secondary screen requires leaning. Score: **9/10**.

## Workspace and devices

There's enough surface for a laptop and phone, but peripherals feel cramped once a full keyboard and mouse are added. Score: **7.5/10**.

## Comfort for the session

With an adjustable lumbar support system and breathable mesh back, long sessions are bearable, though the armrests feel slightly rigid. Score: **8/10**.

## Room presence

The matte black finish integrates well into a home office, but the exposed frame leans industrial rather than refined. Score: **7/10**.

## Audio in the room

The seating position places your ears near typical desk speaker height, but the chair's back reflects some high frequencies. Score: **6/10**.

## Build and stability

The metal frame is solid, but a minor wobble occurs when shifting weight across the seat. Score: **8.5/10**.

## Power and practicality

Integrated routing channels keep leads tidy and power is within easy reach. Score: **9/10**.

## Room dynamics and layout

A modest footprint for what it offers, though it dominates a small room. Score: **7/10**.

## Customisation and adaptability

Lumbar, tilt, and armrests adjust, but replacement parts are hard to source. Score: **8/10**.

## Value for the setup

Strong build and solid scores justify the mid-range price, but cheaper alternatives match much of the comfort. Score: **7.5/10**.

## Verdict

A capable viewing chair with excellent cable management and viewing angle, held back slightly by cramped workspace and rigid armrests. Recommended for single-screen desk setups.
